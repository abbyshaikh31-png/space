What Is Gemini 4 Carbon and What Google Announced at Launch Gemini 4 Carbon is Google&#8217;s top-tier frontier model, opened to all paying API developers and enterprises on November 10, 2026, and built on the same architecture as Gemini 4 Argon with a much larger budget for thinking tokens. Google positions it for cybersecurity and long-running agentic work. Argon stays the default for everyday engineering and knowledge work. The two models are siblings. Google DeepMind&#8217;s model card for Carbon, published November 10, 2026, says it shares Argon&#8217;s architecture, training data and hardware, and differs mainly in how many thinking tokens it spends per task. Argon&#8217;s own card describes a sparse mixture-of-experts transformer with a March 2026 knowledge cutoff, and Carbon carries that same cutoff. Anything that happened after March 2026 has to arrive in the prompt. Google staged the release over four weeks: October 13, 2026: Gemini 4 Argon launched at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. October 28, 2026: Google announced Carbon but handed it only to a group of cyber defenders through the Fairwind Program, with wider access &#8220;coming soon.&#8221; November 10, 2026: Carbon reached general availability for paying API developers and enterprises, and began rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. The positioning is unusually blunt for a flagship launch. Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP of Google DeepMind, wrote in Google&#8217;s November 10, 2026 availability announcement that Carbon is for tasks that stretch a model&#8217;s limits, such as multi-step debugging and open-ended scientific research, and that it gets there by spending more tokens on exploration and verification loops. &#8220;Argon remains the ideal choice for the majority of software engineering and knowledge work, while Carbon is built for tasks that test the limits of a model&#8217;s intelligence.&#8221; Koray Kavukcuoglu, Google DeepMind, November 2026 The other launch headline is /swarm , a preview feature in Google Antigravity. One prompt becomes a coordinator agent with parallel workers in isolated git worktrees and adversarial verifiers that test each change. Google suggests giving Carbon the reasoning-heavy roles (architecture, coordination, auditing) and pairing it with faster, cheaper models for the rest. What struck me is how much of the launch story is orchestration rather than raw model quality. Google is selling a way to spend Carbon&#8217;s expensive reasoning only where it pays off. That framing tells you a lot about the cost profile before you read a single price. What Changed From Gemini 3 to Gemini 4 Carbon The jump from Gemini 3 to Gemini 4 Carbon comes down to four things: a single 1,048,576-token limit shared by input and output, a 65,536-token default output that stretches to the full million with long decode, three selectable thinking levels, and a verification budget far larger than its Argon sibling spends. The Gemini 4 family also drops the split pricing Gemini 3.1 Pro charged above 200,000 tokens. The shared limit catches people out. Per the Gemini 4 Carbon model page in Google AI Studio, updated November 2026, the one-million figure covers prompt and response together. Stuff 900,000 tokens of PDFs into the context and you have roughly 148,000 left for the answer and every thinking token behind it. Inputs span text, image, video, audio and PDF. Output is text only. Carbon&#8217;s additions over Argon are narrow and measurable (all Google-reported, October 2026): Whole-window reasoning: GraphWalks 256k to 1M rises from Argon&#8217;s 84.2% to Carbon&#8217;s 96.2%, a 12-point gain on tasks that traverse the entire context. Terminal work: Terminal-Bench 4.0 climbs from 57.1% to 65.2%, and Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 from 52.9% to 64.8%. Cyber tuning: ExploitGym moves from 42.0% to 54.8%, and SEC-bench Pro from 83.4% to 88.2%. Thinking depth: identical architecture, training data and hardware, with more tokens spent per task on checking its own work. On whole-context GraphWalks 256k to 1M, Carbon reaches 96.2% against Argon&#8217;s 84.2%, with the same architecture and training data. Source: Google DeepMind Gemini 4 Carbon model card, 2026 . Nothing in that list is a new modality or a new architecture. Carbon is Argon told to think longer, then tuned hard on security and long-horizon tasks. That&#8217;s a smaller generational story than the &#8220;Gemini 4&#8221; label suggests, and I&#8217;d rather you know it going in. The pricing shape changed more than the headline numbers. Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, still listed in November 2026, costs $2 input and $12 output up to 200,000 tokens and $4 and $18 above that. Carbon charges one flat rate across the whole million. Through November 30, 2026, a 500,000-token prompt costs half what 3.1 Pro charges for input. From December 1, Carbon&#8217;s $4 input matches 3.1 Pro&#8217;s long-context rate and its $20 output runs $2 higher. Gemini 3.8 Flash stays the cheap option at $0.75 and $3.75 through December 31, 2026, then $1.50 and $7.50. One more change, reported by press at launch: Gemini 3.1 Pro left the Gemini app&#8217;s model picker when Carbon arrived. Google&#8217;s own launch pages don&#8217;t mention the removal. Where Gemini 4 Carbon Is Available and How to Access It via API, Vertex AI and the Gemini App Gemini 4 Carbon is reachable as of November 10, 2026 through five routes: the paid Gemini API under the model ID gemini-4-carbon , Gemini for business, a Google AI Ultra subscription (Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, Antigravity), the Fairwind Program for cyber defenders, and a free six-month scientist programme. Vertex AI is the notable absence from Google&#8217;s launch pages. Gemini API, paid tier. Build in AI Studio or Android Studio against gemini-4-carbon , listed as Stable. The model supports function calling, code execution, computer use, structured outputs, Search and Maps grounding, URL context, file search, and Batch, Flex and Priority inference. It does not support the Live API, image generation or audio generation. Caching is implicit only. Gemini for business. Enterprises get Carbon through the API, the model drop-down, Antigravity, or CodeMender. Google&#8217;s November 10, 2026 post says &#8220;Gemini for business&#8221; and never names Vertex AI. Regional availability goes unstated too. If procurement runs through Vertex, confirm with your account team before anyone writes a migration plan. Google AI Ultra. Subscribers get Carbon in the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Antigravity&#8217;s app, CLI and IDE extensions. The Antigravity team&#8217;s November 10, 2026 post on Carbon previews /swarm there and warns that swarms burn quota much faster than a standard chat. No usage limits are published. An Ultra subscription covers the consumer surfaces and Antigravity. Production API traffic still bills on the paid tier. Fairwind Program. Cyber defenders apply on a rolling basis through the DeepMind Fairwind Program page, which reports over 650 partners in 2026. Governments, critical-infrastructure operators and core technology platforms get priority. Applicants pass background checks, malware creation is prohibited, and access runs through a Cloud account team. Scientists. Google opened applications in October 2026 to give 10,000 scientists six months of free Argon access through Antigravity&#8217;s highest usage tier, and on November 10 extended that to Carbon for current and future participants. Onboarding runs weekly, subject to eligibility, capacity and selection. The friction most teams hit first is the quota question. Antigravity&#8217;s /swarm is the headline feature, and Google has published no ceiling for it. Where Gemini 4 Carbon Still Falls Short Gemini 4 Carbon falls short in four places: it loses to rivals on two agentic benchmarks and sits second on MKA, it is slow and verbose per task, it skips several API features developers expect in 2026, and nearly every headline number comes from Google itself. Start with the scoreboard. On Google&#8217;s own October 2026 model card, GPT-6 Astra beats Carbon on FrontierSWE 2 (65.5% to 61.2%) and Claude Opus 5.5 beats it on OSWorld-2.1 (87.2% to 81.5%). On the Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark, Claude Fable 5.2 holds first at 57% against Carbon&#8217;s 54% as of November 10, 2026. Two of those three tests cover the exact work Google is selling Carbon for: real software engineering and computer use. Then there&#8217;s speed. Artificial Analysis&#8217;s November 2026 measurement of Carbon (High) put time to first answer token at 66.85 seconds against a 12.15-second median across models, with throughput near 80 tokens per second. More than a minute of silence before the first word. For an interactive assistant that is disqualifying. For an overnight agent it is simply the cost of the verification loops. The gaps developers trip on first: No Live API, so no real-time voice or streaming multimodal sessions. No image or audio generation, since output is text only. Implicit caching only, with no way to pin a context and guarantee the discount. No free tier, so every experiment bills from the first token. Read the comparisons with care. The model card&#8217;s Claude column on Terminal-Bench 4.0 is Fable 5.1, while the live MKA leaderboard already includes Fable 5.2. The cyber rows reflect Fairwind Program access run &#8220;without guardrails,&#8221; a configuration no public API customer gets. And the one outside evaluator with launch-day numbers co-built MKA with Google. The safety results are strong, and Google attaches its own asterisk. Who Should Switch to Gemini 4 Carbon and Who Should Wait Security teams, deep-research groups and anyone running agents over a full million-token context should switch to Gemini 4 Carbon now, teams doing routine engineering and knowledge work should stay on Argon, and anyone who cares about latency or the December 1, 2026 price doubling should wait for a second round of independent numbers. Switch now if you fit one of three profiles: Security teams inside the Fairwind Program, where Carbon&#8217;s 2026 lead on finding, exploiting and patching vulnerabilities is widest and the model is the whole point of the programme. Deep-research and long-horizon coding teams whose tasks run for hours or overnight, produce a checkable artifact and can absorb a minute of silence before the first token. Workloads that traverse the entire context window, such as dependency or graph analysis across hundreds of thousands of tokens, where Carbon&#8217;s GraphWalks margin over every rival is the largest on its card. Stay on Argon for everything else. Google says so itself, both models cost the same per token, and Argon spends far fewer of them. A chat assistant, a document classifier, a code-review bot that answers in seconds: none of those improves with a 66-second thinking pause in front of it. Wait if any of these describe you: Your users sit in front of the model and notice latency. Your budget was built on the $2/$10 rate and nobody has modelled the $4/$20 bill that starts December 1, 2026. Your procurement needs independent benchmark coverage, which on November 10, 2026 amounts to one evaluator that co-built one of the tests. You buy through Vertex AI and Google has yet to confirm Carbon there. Here&#8217;s the part I&#8217;d push on. Price parity through November 30 makes the trial almost free. Take the hardest task on your backlog, run it through both models with token counts logged, and let the bill and the output decide. Three weeks is enough. Frequently Asked Questions About Gemini 4 Carbon Is Gemini 4 Carbon free to use? No. The Gemini API has no free tier for Carbon, and the paid rate is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through November 30, 2026, doubling to $4 and $20 on December 1. Consumers reach it through a Google AI Ultra subscription. Eligible scientists can apply for six months of free access in Antigravity, and cyber defenders can apply to the Fairwind Program. What is the Gemini 4 Carbon context window? Gemini 4 Carbon has a 1,048,576-token context window that input and output share, per Google&#8217;s model page updated November 2026. Default maximum output is 65,536 tokens, extendable to the full 1,048,576 with long decode. Thinking tokens count against that output. Gemini 4 Carbon vs Argon: which should I use? Use Argon for most software engineering and knowledge work, and Carbon when a task needs long verification loops: vulnerability research, whole-context graph traversal, or bug fixes that run for days. The two share one architecture, one training set and one per-token price in 2026. Carbon spends more tokens per task, so it costs more per job even at the same rate. When does the $2/$10 introductory price end? The introductory price ends November 30, 2026. From December 1, 2026, Standard costs $4 input and $20 output per million tokens, Batch and Flex cost $2 and $10, Priority costs $7.20 and $36, and cached input rises from $0.10 to $0.20. Does Gemini 4 Carbon support the Live API or image generation? No. Google&#8217;s November 2026 model page lists the Live API, image generation and audio generation as unsupported. Carbon accepts text, image, video, audio and PDF input and returns text only. It does support function calling, code execution, computer use, structured outputs, Search and Maps grounding, URL context and file search. What is the Fairwind Program? The Fairwind Program is Google DeepMind&#8217;s access channel for cybersecurity defenders, and it received Carbon on October 28, 2026, two weeks before general availability. Applications are rolling, applicants pass background checks, malware creation is prohibited, and governments, critical-infrastructure operators and core technology platforms get priority. The programme reported over 650 partners in 2026, with access handled through a Cloud account team. Is Gemini 4 Carbon available on Vertex AI? Google&#8217;s November 10, 2026 launch materials list the Gemini API in AI Studio and &#8220;Gemini for business&#8221; without naming Vertex AI, and they give no regional availability. Treat Vertex access as unconfirmed and ask your Google Cloud account team before planning a migration. What is Gemini 4 Carbon&#8217;s knowledge cutoff? March 2026, according to the Gemini 4 Carbon model card published November 10, 2026. Anything that happened after that date has to be supplied in the prompt or retrieved through grounding.
