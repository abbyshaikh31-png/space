The Lion

Panthera leo is the only truly social cat. Prides of related females and their cubs, with coalitions of males, define its life in the savannas of Africa and a small population in India's Gir Forest.

Females do most of the cooperative hunting and raise cubs communally. Males defend territory and the pride against rival coalitions, and they eat first at a kill.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons, Kalahari lion cub, four months old.

Conservation Status

The IUCN lists the lion as Vulnerable. Habitat loss, conflict with livestock herders, and declining prey populations are the main threats across Africa.

Protected areas, community-based conservation, and compensation schemes for livestock losses are the most widely used responses.

Key Takeaways

Lions are the most social wild cats, and their survival depends on large connected landscapes, healthy prey populations, and coexistence with people.
